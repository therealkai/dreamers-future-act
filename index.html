Letter to a Representative

Background on the issue and why this legislation is needed.

Oct 30, 2026

Dear Representative Peters,

I am writing to express my support for the Childhood Roots and American Futures Act (CRAFT Act), which would establish a pathway to legal permanent residency for individuals who entered the United States at age 12 or younger. Many of these individuals have grown up in the United States, attended American schools, and built their lives in American communities. However, without a reliable pathway to permanent residency, they may face uncertainty about their futures, education, and employment opportunities.

The CRAFT Act would address this issue by allowing eligible applicants to obtain provisional legal status for five years before applying for permanent residency. Applicants would undergo background checks, demonstrate basic English proficiency, and participate in American society through employment, education, training, or other approved pathways. The bill would also provide reasonable exceptions for individuals facing disabilities, serious illnesses, caregiving responsibilities, or financial hardship.

Although implementing this legislation would require significant government funding for staff, background checks, and application processing, participants could contribute to the economy through employment, taxes, and entrepreneurship. These contributions may help offset some of the program's costs over time.

I urge you to consider the CRAFT Act as a way to provide eligible individuals with greater stability while promoting public safety, economic participation, and stronger communities. Thank you for your time and consideration.

Sincerely,

Kai & Jun

The Bill

The full text of the Childhood Roots and American Futures Act (CRAFT Act).

A Bill

Childhood Roots and American Futures Act (CRAFT Act)

Introduced by Kai & Jun

Purpose & Goals

The purpose of the CRAFT Act is to create a pathway to legal permanent residency for individuals who entered the United States at age 12 or younger and have built their lives in the country.

Many of these individuals have grown up in American communities, attended American schools, and developed strong connections to the United States. This Act aims to give eligible individuals a chance to build stable futures while encouraging education, employment, and responsible participation in society.

The goals of this Act are to:

  • Provide eligible individuals with a pathway to permanent residency and eventual citizenship.
  • Protect public safety through background checks.
  • Encourage English proficiency, education, employment, and community involvement.
  • Allow reasonable exceptions for people facing serious personal or financial difficulties.
  • Strengthen the American workforce and economy.
  • Create a fair application process with financial assistance for those who need it.

Section 1: Eligibility Requirements

  1. Age of Entry. Applicants must have entered the United States at age 12 or younger.
  2. Residency. Applicants must prove that they have lived continuously in the United States for a period set by the administering agency. Reasonable exceptions may be made for temporary absences or special circumstances.
  3. Background Checks. Applicants must complete criminal and national-security background checks. Decisions must consider the seriousness of any offense, the applicant's age at the time, evidence of rehabilitation, and other relevant circumstances.
  4. English Proficiency. Applicants must demonstrate basic English proficiency by a stage set by the administering agency. Language-learning resources and reasonable accommodations must be available to those who need them.
  5. Medical Requirements. Applicants must complete any medical examinations required by law and meet applicable public-health requirements.
  6. Application and Fees. Applicants must submit an application and supporting documents and pay the required fee. Fee waivers or reductions must be available to eligible individuals experiencing financial hardship.

Section 2: Education, Employment, and Community Participation

  1. Participation Requirement. Applicants must demonstrate that they are contributing to American society through at least one of the following options:
    • Employment: Working legally, being self-employed, or participating in another recognized form of work.
    • Education or Training: Attending or completing college, vocational school, an apprenticeship, or another recognized career-training program.
    • Military Service: Completing qualifying voluntary military service, if eligible.
    • Alternative Eligibility: Meeting other requirements approved by the administering agency if disability, serious illness, caregiving responsibilities, financial hardship, or other exceptional circumstances prevent an applicant from meeting the standard requirements.
  2. Flexible Requirements. Applicants shall not be required to follow the same path. The administering agency must consider each person's circumstances when deciding whether they meet the requirements.
  3. Proof of Participation. Applicants may provide documents such as employment records, school transcripts, training certificates, or military service records to show that they meet the requirements.

Section 3: Provisional Status and Permanent Residency

  1. Provisional Status. Applicants who meet the requirements shall receive provisional legal status for five years after their applications are approved. This status shall allow them to live legally in the United States and qualify for work authorization under rules established by the administering agency.
  2. Following the Law. During the five-year period, participants must follow applicable laws and avoid serious criminal or national-security violations that would disqualify them. Decisions must consider the seriousness of the conduct, the person's age at the time, evidence of rehabilitation, and other relevant circumstances.
  3. Continued Participation. During the five-year period, participants must continue to demonstrate meaningful participation in American society through employment, education, training, qualifying voluntary service, or another approved option.
  4. Exceptions. Participants who cannot meet these requirements because of disability, serious illness, caregiving responsibilities, financial hardship, or other exceptional circumstances may qualify through alternative requirements established by the administering agency.
  5. Permanent Residency Application. Before the five-year period ends, participants may apply for lawful permanent resident status, commonly known as a green card. The administering agency shall review their records and approve those who meet the requirements of this Act.
  6. Fair Review Process. Applicants must receive notice if their application or status may be denied or terminated and must have a meaningful opportunity to respond. The administering agency shall establish procedures for appeals, extensions, and special circumstances.
  7. Pathway to Citizenship. Individuals who receive permanent residency under this Act may later apply for United States citizenship if they meet the requirements of federal naturalization law.

Section 4: Administration and Funding

  1. Administration. The Department of Homeland Security, through the appropriate immigration authorities and other relevant agencies, shall oversee the implementation of this Act.
  2. Implementation. The administering agency shall establish application procedures, eligibility rules, documentation requirements, and timelines for processing applications.
  3. Staffing and Costs. Implementing this Act will require significant funding for additional personnel, application processing, background checks, medical reviews, information systems, language-learning resources, and appeals.
  4. Government Funding. Congress shall provide funding as needed to carry out this Act. The administering agency shall regularly report to Congress on program costs, application numbers, processing times, and approval and denial rates.
  5. Economic Benefits. Individuals who receive legal status under this Act may contribute to the economy by working legally, paying taxes, starting businesses, and spending money in their communities. Their tax contributions and economic activity may help offset the costs of running the program over time. However, the amount of revenue generated will depend on factors such as employment, earnings, tax payments, and the number of participants. These contributions may reduce government costs, but they cannot guarantee that the program will pay for itself.
  6. Affordable Applications. Application fees shall be set according to applicable law. Fee waivers or reductions must be available to eligible applicants who cannot afford the full cost.
  7. Oversight. The administering agency shall take steps to prevent fraud, protect applicants' personal information, and ensure that applications are reviewed fairly and consistently.

Section 5: Final Provisions

  1. Rules and Regulations. The administering agency shall establish the rules needed to carry out this Act within a reasonable period after it becomes law.
  2. Fair Treatment. Each application shall be reviewed individually. Applicants who qualify for an approved alternative pathway shall not be denied solely because they cannot meet a standard education or employment requirement.
  3. Existing Immigration Laws. Individuals receiving benefits under this Act must still meet all other applicable immigration and naturalization requirements unless this Act specifically provides otherwise.
  4. Severability. If any part of this Act is found to be invalid, the remaining parts shall continue to apply whenever possible.
  5. Effective Date. This Act shall take effect when it becomes law. Applications shall be accepted once the administering agency has established the necessary rules and procedures.

Conclusion

The Childhood Roots and American Futures Act provides a structured pathway to permanent residency and eventual citizenship for eligible individuals who entered the United States at age 12 or younger. By recognizing the lives they have built in American communities, the Act offers an opportunity for greater stability while encouraging education, employment, and community participation.

Although the program will require significant initial funding for staff, background checks, and application processing, participants may help offset these costs through taxes, employment, business creation, and other economic contributions over time.

Through clear requirements, fair reviews, and reasonable exceptions for people facing difficult circumstances, the CRAFT Act aims to balance public safety, economic growth, and the opportunity for eligible individuals to build their futures in the United States.
